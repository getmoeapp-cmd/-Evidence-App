# MELANOCORTIN RECEPTOR AGONIST
# PT-141 (Bremelanotide)

One of the few in the catalog with FDA approval — but for a very specific indication: hypoactive sexual desire disorder in premenopausal women. Male use, which is nearly the whole market, is off-label and stopped at phase 2.

**LEVEL A · FOR ITS APPROVED INDICATION**
Randomized phase 3, published, FDA-approved (2019). For use in men: LEVEL B, unapproved.

## HOW IT'S USED ON THE LABEL
At least 45 minutes before anticipated sexual activity. No more than one dose per 24 hours and no more than ~8 doses per month. In the trials, average use was 2 to 3 times per month. More frequent use increases the risk of hyperpigmentation.

## WHAT'S CLAIMED · WHAT'S BEEN MEASURED

**Increases sexual desire in premenopausal women with HSDD — LEVEL A**
Phase 3: statistically significant improvement in the desire domain versus placebo. The absolute difference is modest — real, measured and approved, but not spectacular.

**Treats erectile dysfunction in men — LEVEL B**
Phase 2 data, including PDE5 non-responders. Never taken to phase 3 and never submitted for approval.

**Acts on desire, not on blood flow — LEVEL A · mechanism**
This is the real difference from Viagra/Cialis: it acts in the central nervous system via melanocortin receptors.

**Works in postmenopausal women — NO DATA**
All the phase 3 evidence is in premenopausal women.

## THE SOURCE

**IN PLAIN LANGUAGE**
Two phase 3 trials with more than 1,200 premenopausal women with hypoactive sexual desire showed a significant improvement on desire scales versus placebo. The magnitude of the change was modest, and on that basis the FDA approved the drug in 2019.

**LIMITATIONS**
The mean effect is small in absolute terms, and nausea drove discontinuations in the trials. Strictly premenopausal population. Manufacturer-funded.

**ORIGINAL CITATION**
RECONNECT program — Bremelanotide for HSDD in premenopausal women
Obstetrics & Gynecology, 2019
Two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials · ~1,247 participants

## CONTRAINDICATIONS AND INTERACTIONS
There is an approved label here.

**Uncontrolled hypertension or cardiovascular disease — DO NOT USE / CAUTION**
It produces transient blood pressure elevations. Approved product label

**Pregnancy — DO NOT USE**
Approved product label

**Nausea — MAIN ADVERSE EFFECT**
The most frequent in the trials and the most common cause of discontinuation.

**Hyperpigmentation (darkening of skin or gums) — LABEL SIGNAL**
Higher risk with frequent use; may not be reversible. This is the reason for the monthly limit.

**Oral naltrexone — INTERACTION**
May reduce its levels. Approved product label

## REGULATORY STATUS

**UNITED STATES · FDA** — Approved (Vyleesi, June 2019) for acquired, generalized hypoactive sexual desire disorder in premenopausal women. No approved generic. Any use in men is off-label.

**EUROPE · EMA / CANADA / UNITED KINGDOM** — No marketing authorization.

**COLOMBIA · INVIMA / MEXICO · COFEPRIS** — No known marketing authorization. What circulates as "research PT-141" is not the approved product and passes through no pharmaceutical control.
