## WHAT'S CLAIMED · WHAT'S BEEN MEASURED

**Kills bacteria, including resistant strains — LEVEL C**
Very well demonstrated in a laboratory dish. Translation to humans runs into three known problems: it degrades quickly under proteases, it is cytotoxic at concentrations near the antimicrobial range, and it is expensive to produce.

**Breaks up biofilms — LEVEL C**
In vitro.

**Heals wounds / improves skin — LEVEL C**
Preclinical and skin barrier data; no conclusive trials.

**Treats chronic infections in people — NO DATA**
This is the use being sold and the one with no trials.

**Critical point:** LL-37 is not simply "the body's antibiotic." When it is overexpressed or in the wrong place, it participates in the inflammation of psoriasis, lupus, rheumatoid arthritis and several cancers. It is a documented double-edged sword.

---

## THE SOURCE

**IN PLAIN LANGUAGE**
LL-37 is the only cathelicidin humans produce, released by neutrophils and epithelial cells in response to infection or injury. In the laboratory it destroys bacterial membranes with notable efficiency. That same mechanism, misdirected, damages the body's own cells — which is why no medicine based on it exists yet.

**LIMITATIONS**
Decades of mechanistic research have produced no approval. The gap is not one of scientific interest: it is safety and stability.

**ANCHOR CITATION**
Reviews of the cathelicidin family and LL-37 modifications (2025–2026)
Reported limitations: low proteolytic stability, cytotoxicity, production cost

## CONTRAINDICATIONS AND SIGNALS
No label exists. The following are the concerns the FDA itself named when keeping it restricted:

**Effects on male fertility — SIGNAL STATED BY THE FDA**

**Protumorigenic effects in some tissues — SIGNAL STATED BY THE FDA**

**Autoimmune or inflammatory disease (psoriasis, lupus, rheumatoid arthritis) — STRONG CAUTION**
The peptide is implicated in the pathophysiology of these conditions.

**Cytotoxicity — MECHANISM SIGNAL**
The concentrations that kill bacteria approach those that damage human cells.

**Pregnancy and breastfeeding — NO DATA AT ALL**

## REGULATORY STATUS

**UNITED STATES · FDA** — Not approved. Placed in Category 2 in 2023 and **kept there**: it was not among the peptides released in the 2024–2026 reclassification, alongside melanotan II, GHRP-2, GHRP-6 and others. That is an important distinction within the catalog.

**COLOMBIA · INVIMA / MEXICO · COFEPRIS** — No marketing authorization. Circulates as a "research reagent."
