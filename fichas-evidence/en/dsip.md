# SLEEP PEPTIDE (EMIDELTIDE)
# DSIP

It is called "delta sleep-inducing peptide," and that name does almost all the marketing work. The human evidence is from the 1970s and 80s, small, inconsistent — and it is not even clear that it induces sleep.

**LEVEL C**
Old, small human studies with inconsistent results. No modern randomized trial.

## WHAT'S CLAIMED · WHAT'S BEEN MEASURED

**Induces deep (delta) sleep — LEVEL C · INCONSISTENT**
That is its name, not its result. Human studies did not consistently confirm that it increases slow-wave sleep. Later work even questioned whether it is a sleep peptide at all.

**Reduces chronic pain — LEVEL C**
Reported in small old case series.

**Helps with alcohol and opioid withdrawal — LEVEL C**
The line of research with the most signal in the old studies; never developed.

**Reduces stress / normalizes blood pressure — NO CONTROLLED DATA**

## THE SOURCE

**IN PLAIN LANGUAGE**
The compound was isolated in 1977 after researchers observed that a blood extract from rabbits in induced sleep produced delta sleep in other animals. Attempts to reproduce that effect in humans, decades ago, gave uneven results, and the research stopped. What is sold today rests on that name and on anecdotal reports.

**LIMITATIONS**
Forty years without a modern trial is itself a data point: no group has considered it worth confirming.

**ANCHOR CITATION**
Original DSIP literature (isolation 1977; human studies from the 1970s–80s)
No modern randomized trials published

## REGULATORY STATUS

**UNITED STATES · FDA** — Not approved. Restricted from compounding in 2023 (Category 2); in 2026 it came under compounding committee review as "emideltide." Review is not approval.

**COLOMBIA · INVIMA / MEXICO · COFEPRIS** — No marketing authorization. Circulates as a "research reagent."
