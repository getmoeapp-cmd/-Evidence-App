# THYMIC IMMUNOMODULATORY PEPTIDE
# Thymosin Alpha-1 (Tα1)

The best-validated peptide in the catalog that almost nobody knows: it is an approved medicine in more than 35 countries since the 1990s — just not in the United States. Its absence from the FDA is commercial, not scientific.

**LEVEL A · FOR ITS APPROVED INDICATION**
Published randomized trials in chronic hepatitis B, with regulatory approval in dozens of countries. For the "wellness" uses being sold: LEVEL C.

## WHAT'S CLAIMED · WHAT'S BEEN MEASURED

**Treats chronic hepatitis B — LEVEL A**
Approved indication in 35+ countries. Phase 3 with sustained virologic response superior to placebo.

**Adjunct in oncology and immunosuppression — LEVEL B**
Established clinical use in several countries (China, Italy and others) with trials of variable quality.

**Improves vaccine response — LEVEL B**
An approved indication in some markets.

**Sepsis and COVID-19 — MIXED RESULTS**
Pandemic-era studies with inconsistent signals; nothing conclusive.

**"Boosts the immune system" in healthy people — NO DATA**
All of its evidence is in populations with disease. It is a modulator, not a generic booster — and in healthy people it was never tested.

## THE SOURCE

**IN PLAIN LANGUAGE**
Randomized trials in patients with chronic hepatitis B showed that a significantly larger share of the treated group achieved sustained control of the virus versus control. On that basis it was approved as a medicine (Zadaxin) in dozens of countries.

**LIMITATIONS**
The absence of approval in the US and centrally in Europe reflects commercial development decisions more than a safety rejection. Even so, without FDA/EMA review there is no Western label standard for its use.

**ORIGINAL CITATION**
Thymalfasin (thymosin alpha-1) trials in chronic hepatitis B
Pivotal program · randomized and controlled
Approved as Zadaxin in 35+ countries

---

## CONTRAINDICATIONS AND INTERACTIONS
An international label exists; a US one does not.

**Active autoimmune disease — CAUTION**
It modulates the immune response upward: theoretical risk of flare.

**Organ transplant / immunosuppression — STRONG CAUTION**
May work against the goal of immunosuppressive treatment.

**Pregnancy and breastfeeding — NO DATA**

**Tolerability profile — CLEAN**
Decades of clinical use with a low adverse-event rate; the most common issue is an injection site reaction.

## REGULATORY STATUS

**INTERNATIONAL** — Approved as Zadaxin/thymalfasin in more than 35 countries (China, Italy, India, the Philippines, several in Latin America and the Middle East) for chronic hepatitis B and oncology immunomodulation.

**UNITED STATES · FDA** — Not approved. Holds orphan drug designation for some indications. Restricted from compounding in 2023; included in the 2026 reclassification.

**EUROPE · EMA** — No centralized approval, despite clinical use in Italy.

**COLOMBIA · INVIMA / MEXICO · COFEPRIS** — Verify local registration: approval elsewhere does not imply registration here. What is sold as "research grade" is not the approved product.
